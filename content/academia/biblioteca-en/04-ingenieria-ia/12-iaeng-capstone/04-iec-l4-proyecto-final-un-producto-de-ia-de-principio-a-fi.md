## Project brief

This is the close of the area. You're going to take an AI product from proposal to measured deployment, for a real client or your own agency.

It isn't a technical exercise: it's the full journey as it happens on an engagement. Code is half of it; the other half is scoping, measuring, deploying and being able to explain what you built.

Pick a problem with data that actually exists and that you have access to. Keep scope small and write the success criterion before starting.

## Tasks
- Write the full proposal and validate it with someone before writing any code
- Build the evaluation set with real cases, including ones that must escalate
- Develop by measuring: every change is justified by its effect on evaluation
- Deploy in stages, starting with internal use
- Prepare the presentation with a non-technical client in mind

## Deliverables
- Proposal with problem, scope, what the system does NOT do, measurable success criterion and risk analysis
- Working, deployed system with its model version pinned
- Evaluation set of at least 40 cases, with per-stage and end-to-end results
- Monthly cost calculation at expected volume, with the proposed billing model
- Full traces, spend limits and alerts configured
- Tested kill switch that returns flow to the manual process
- User feedback wired into the evaluation set
- Client handoff document: what it does, what it doesn't, how it's operated and what maintenance covers
- 10-minute presentation covering problem, solution, numbers and limits

## Rubric
- The success criterion was written before building and is verified by evaluation
- The proposal explicitly states what stays out of scope
- Evaluation measures by stage and the report explains what changed based on its results
- There is a monthly cost calculation with its volume assumption and a billing model consistent with it
- The model version is pinned and there's a written retirement plan
- Spend limits actually cut off: demonstrated by triggering them
- The kill switch was tested and is documented
- The handoff document is understandable to a non-technical reader
- The presentation states clearly what the system does NOT do and what happens when it's wrong

## Discussion questions
- If the provider doubled the model price tomorrow, is your project still profitable? What would you change?
- What part of the system would a person have to do if AI had to be shut off completely tomorrow?

## Tip

The presentation matters as much as the system. A client doesn't buy architecture: they buy a solved problem, with its limits explained. Practice saying out loud what your system doesn't do and what happens when it fails, because those are the two questions they're going to ask.

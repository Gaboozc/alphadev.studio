# Working with AI — Syllabus

**Semester 0 · Shared Core · Course 1 of 3**
**Length:** 10 weeks · **Workload:** 6 hours a week (2 h guided reading, 3 h practice, 1 h reflection and review) · **Total:** 60 hours
**Format:** self-paced, with weekly submissions and a final project reviewed by a person.

## Description

This course teaches you to work with language models (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and similar tools) with a professional method instead of trial and error. It starts with what almost nobody explains: what a language model really is, what it does when it "answers," and why it sometimes makes things up. The technique is built on that foundation: the anatomy of a complete prompt, techniques that measurably improve results, context management and, finally, how to turn AI into a working system with verification, clear costs and ethical limits.

The course doesn't teach "hacks" tied to a specific model version. Models change every few months; the principles in this course (give context, specify the output, show examples, verify and measure) outlast those changes.

## Prerequisites

- Nothing technical. You need to know how to use a browser and a word processor.
- A free account with at least one AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini). You don't need to pay to pass the course.
- Recommended: take it alongside *Professional Writing*, because writing good prompts is, above all, writing clearly.

## Learning objectives

By the end, you'll be able to:

1. **Explain** in your own words how a language model generates text (tokens, prediction, context, temperature) and work out from that why it hallucinates and why its answers vary.
2. **Write** complete prompts with the seven components of a professional prompt and justify each one.
3. **Apply** improvement techniques (few-shot examples, step-by-step reasoning, breaking work into subtasks, structured output) and choose the right one for each task.
4. **Manage** the context of a conversation: when to continue, when to start over and where to place the important information.
5. **Evaluate** the quality of an answer with explicit criteria and verify the facts before using them.
6. **Design** an AI workflow for a real professional task, with human checkpoints, an estimate of time saved and costs.
7. **Recognize** the ethical, legal and privacy limits of using AI (client data, authorship, bias).

## Assessment

| Component | Weight | Grading |
|---|---|---|
| U1–U4 quizzes (multiple choice) | 20% | **Automatic** |
| U1–U4 quizzes (short answer) | 10% | **Human** with a guidance key (or AI with human review) |
| Graded exercises (submitted with evidence) | 20% | **Mixed**: level 1 exercises have a single answer and are graded automatically; levels 2–3 are reviewed by a person against the reference solution |
| U1–U4 reflections | 15% | **Human** with a rubric |
| Final project | 35% | **Mixed**: automatic deliverables checklist, quality graded with a human rubric |

**Passing:** 70% weighted, and at least 60% on the final project.

## AI use policy for this course

You'll use AI all the time in this course, and that's fine. What isn't acceptable is turning in an AI answer as if it were your own reflection. Reflections and the final project analysis assess your judgment: if AI helped you write, say so in one line at the end ("I used AI to…"). Not disclosing it counts as an integrity violation.

## Materials

- Study guides for each unit (in this folder).
- Optional reference readings:
  - Brown et al. (2020), *Language Models are Few-Shot Learners* (NeurIPS). The paper that popularized learning from examples inside the prompt.
  - Wei et al. (2022), *Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models* (NeurIPS).
  - Liu et al. (2024), *Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts* (TACL, vol. 12).
  - The prompting best-practice documentation from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google (updated often; check the current version).

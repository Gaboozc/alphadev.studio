**2.1 (reference)**
> You're a social media manager for neighborhood coffee shops. My coffee shop, Slow Grind, is in Portland, Oregon, open 7 a.m. to 2 p.m., and this week we're launching house-made cornbread. Write the caption for an Instagram post announcing it. Our followers are mostly neighbors aged 25 to 40 who come in for breakfast before work. Goal: get them to come try it this week. Friendly tone, no hype; 60 words max, one emoji at most, no generic hashtags like #food. Give me 3 options, each with a one-line photo suggestion.
Graded on whether all seven components are present and the context is specific.

**2.2 (reference)** Verifiable: ≤ 60 words (count); ≤ 1 emoji (count); mentions the product and the hours/week (search). Judgment: friendly tone without hype (no empty superlatives like "the best in the world").

**2.3 (reference)** Graded on: a complete table, one change per row, specific observations (not just "it got better"), a justified conclusion. Most often, context or constraints produce the biggest improvement; any conclusion consistent with the evidence is valid.

**3.1 (reference)** Problems: (1) generic and interchangeable → missing context (which agency, what kind of sites, for whom); (2) clichés ("innovative solutions," "next level") → missing constraints; (3) it talks about the agency, not the client's outcome → missing audience and goal. Corrected prompt: must include a specific business, the ideal client, a specific benefit, a ban on clichés, length and format.

**3.2 (reference)** True: the role changes vocabulary, tone and approach, and can make the answer better organized and better suited to the audience. Not true: it doesn't add knowledge or verify facts; an answer with an expert role can contain the same errors, just stated with more authority, which is more dangerous. Reliability comes from providing sources and verifying.

# Professional and Persuasive Writing — Syllabus

**Semester 0 · Shared Core · Course 3 of 3**
**Length:** 10 weeks · **Workload:** 6 hours a week · **Total:** 60 hours

> **About the English edition.** This course was **rewritten for English**, not translated word for word. Clarity rules, wordiness patterns, examples, legal references (FTC instead of Mexico's consumer protection law), formats (US dates, USD, sales tax) and UI conventions (sentence case, register) are the ones that apply to English. Structure, objectives, piece ids, assessment and answer keys match the Spanish edition one to one.

## Description

In any digital career, a huge part of the work is writing: an email to a client, the label on a button, a proposal, a product description, an error message, a report. Writing clearly gets you understood; writing with intent gets people to act. This course teaches both, ethically.

The course brings together material that was spread across three different branches of the academy (copywriting in Content & SEO, UX writing in Design and proposals in Content & SEO) and turns it into a shared foundation for developers, designers and marketers. It starts with **clarity** (U1), moves on to honest **persuasion** (U2), applies both to **interface** copy (U3) and finishes with the two documents that move the most money at an agency: the **proposal** and the **professional email** (U4).

## Prerequisites

None. It's recommended to have taken, or to take alongside, *Working with AI* (AI is used to generate and review drafts) and *Problem Solving and Critical Thinking* (U2 revisits biases and the ethics of persuasion; U4, anchoring).

## Learning objectives

1. **Analyze** the reader and the purpose before writing, and **structure** text for how people actually read on screens.
2. **Write** clearly: short sentences, active voice, concrete words, inverted-pyramid structure.
3. **Turn** features into benefits and **write** a specific value proposition.
4. **Apply** principles of persuasion (Cialdini) and copy formulas (AIDA, PAS, FAB, BAB), **telling** persuasion apart from manipulation.
5. **Write** clear, useful, consistent interface microcopy (buttons, errors, empty states, forms).
6. **Put together** a complete proposal and effective professional emails.
7. **Review** and **edit** your own and AI-generated text with a checklist.

## Assessment

| Component | Weight | Grading |
|---|---|---|
| U1–U4 quizzes, multiple choice | 20% | **Automatic** |
| U1–U4 quizzes, short answer | 10% | **Human** with a key |
| Graded exercises | 20% | **Mixed** (level 1 automatic; levels 2–3 human) |
| U1–U4 reflections | 15% | **Human** with a rubric |
| Final project | 35% | **Mixed** (automatic checklist + human rubric) |

Passing: 70% weighted and 60% on the project.

> **A note on human grading.** Persuasive writing doesn't have a single right answer. The keys give reference answers and criteria; the grader scores against the rubric, not by how closely an answer resembles the reference.

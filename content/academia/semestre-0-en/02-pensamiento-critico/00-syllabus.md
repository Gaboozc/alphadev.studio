# Problem Solving and Critical Thinking — Syllabus

**Semester 0 · Shared Core · Course 2 of 3**
**Length:** 11 weeks · **Workload:** 6 hours per week · **Total:** 66 hours
**New course:** it isn't derived from any existing Academia lesson; it was written from scratch for the Shared Core. (The *Terminal and Git* course that used to hold this slot moves to the first semester of the Software Engineering and AI program.)

## Description

Every program in the Academia (software, design, marketing) comes down, day to day, to the same thing: someone brings a poorly defined problem ("sales dropped," "the app is slow," "the campaign isn't working"), and you have to understand what's going on, why, and what to do, with incomplete information and little time. This course teaches you to do that with a method.

It covers the full cycle: **defining** the problem well, **breaking it down**, looking for **causes** with hypotheses and evidence, **reasoning** without falling into fallacies, recognizing your own **biases** and evaluating **sources** (including AI answers), and **deciding** when there's no certainty. Every case comes from digital work: agencies, software products, e-commerce, campaigns.

## Prerequisites

None. Basic arithmetic (percentages, averages). We recommend taking it alongside *Working with AI*, because Unit 4 applies source evaluation to model outputs.

## Learning objectives

1. **Frame** a problem precisely (symptom, gap, scope, constraints, success criterion) and distinguish it from its symptoms and from premature solutions.
2. **Break down** a problem into mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive parts.
3. **Identify root causes** with structured techniques (five whys, Ishikawa diagram) and **test** hypotheses against evidence, distinguishing correlation from causation.
4. **Analyze** arguments (premises, conclusion, type of reasoning) and **spot** the most common fallacies in professional settings.
5. **Recognize** cognitive biases in yourself and others, and apply countermeasures.
6. **Evaluate** the reliability of information sources, including the outputs of AI tools, with explicit methods (lateral reading, SIFT, CRAAP).
7. **Make and document** decisions under uncertainty using probability estimates, expected value, reversibility, weighted matrices and pre-mortems.

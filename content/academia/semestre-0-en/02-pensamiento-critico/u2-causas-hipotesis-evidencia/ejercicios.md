# Unit 2 — Tiered Exercises

Solutions in `../claves/u2-soluciones-ejercicios.md`.

## Level 1

**1.1** (auto-graded) Which of these are falsifiable hypotheses? (there may be more than one)
a) "The audience doesn't feel the brand."
b) "Downloads fell because since version 3.2 the app won't open on Android 12."
c) "Things are going badly because of the team's bad energy."
d) "Emails have been going to spam since we changed the sending domain on May 5."

**1.2** (auto-graded) Identify the type of alternative explanation (reverse causation, confounding, chance): "Stores with more employees sell more; let's hire more employees at every store."

**1.3** (auto-graded) Rank from strongest to weakest: (a) a survey of 200 customers; (b) a randomized A/B test with 20,000 visits; (c) a customer's comment on social media; (d) a before/after comparison with a control group.

## Level 2

**2.1 (reviewed with a rubric)** For "ad cost per acquired customer doubled last month," generate 4 hypotheses with: what would disprove it, cost to test, estimated likelihood, and the order in which you'd investigate them.

**2.2 (reviewed with a rubric)** Apply the five whys to: "A client received an invoice with the wrong amount." Reach a systemic cause and propose a lasting action.

**2.3 (reviewed with a rubric)** Build an Ishikawa diagram (5 categories, at least 2 causes per category) for "the app's sign-up abandonment rate is 60%."

## Level 3

**3.1 (reviewed with a rubric)** A report says: "Since we started posting on LinkedIn, requests for proposals have risen 40%. LinkedIn works." Write a 200–250-word analysis with at least three alternative explanations, and design a way to test the real effect.

**3.2 (reviewed with a rubric)** Design an A/B test to check whether a "Book a call" button converts better than "Request a proposal": hypothesis, primary metric, assignment, duration, what result would change your mind, and what could invalidate the test.

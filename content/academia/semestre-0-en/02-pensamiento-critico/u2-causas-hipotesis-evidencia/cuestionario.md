# Unit 2 — Quiz

Key in `../claves/u2-clave-cuestionario.md`. **Part A [AUTO]** · **Part B [HUMAN]**

1. What sets a hypothesis apart from an opinion is that:
   a) It's optimistic and proposes a concrete improvement. b) It's written down and signed by the team in charge. c) It comes from an expert with years in the industry. d) You can say which observation would disprove it.
2. Why generate several hypotheses before investigating?
   a) So the report is longer and more complete. b) Because the method requires at least three hypotheses. c) So you don't only look for evidence supporting the first one. d) To spread the blame across several departments.
3. The five whys are historically associated with:
   a) Toyota b) NASA c) Apple d) Google
4. In the five whys, ending at "because Anna made a mistake" is an error because:
   a) Anna might get upset and stop cooperating. b) It doesn't ask what allowed the error to escalate. c) It's not an error: finding who's responsible is enough. d) That's fewer than five whys, and there must be five.
5. The Ishikawa diagram is mainly used to:
   a) Prove which of the causes is the real one. b) Measure the financial impact of each cause. c) Assign a person responsible to each category. d) Organize possible causes to generate hypotheses.
6. "Months with more posts have more sales." A plausible confounding variable is:
   a) The company's name in each post. b) The time of year (e.g., December). c) The color of the logo in the posts. d) None: more posts cause more sales.
7. The strongest way to get closer to causation is:
   a) The opinion of the most experienced director. b) A large survey of current customers. c) An experiment with random assignment. d) A very convincing anecdote from a customer.
8. A 50% increase in conversion measured on 8 visits:
   a) Doesn't support a conclusion; the sample is too small. b) Proves the change works very well. c) Is impossible and must be a data error. d) Is conclusive because the increase is large.
9. Before concluding that "traffic dropped," it's worth checking:
   a) The client's opinion about the traffic. b) Nothing: if the data says so, traffic dropped. c) Whether the way traffic is measured changed. d) The color of the homepage that month.
10. Anecdotes are useful for:
    a) Generating hypotheses. b) Nothing at all. c) Confirming hypotheses. d) Calculating impact.

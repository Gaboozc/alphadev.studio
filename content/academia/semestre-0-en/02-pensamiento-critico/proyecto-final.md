# Final Project — Case Resolution Report

**Week 11 · 35% of the grade · Mixed assessment**

## Brief

Apply the integrated method (Unit 5, section 7) to a real or realistic problem from digital work and turn in a **resolution report** that someone with decision-making authority can read and use to decide.

Choose one of two tracks:

- **Track A — Your own case:** a problem from your job, a client, a personal project or an organization you know (with real or anonymized data).
- **Track B — Provided case:** use the case below.

### Provided case: "North Studio"

North Studio is a 9-person digital agency. Over the last two quarters:
- Revenue held steady, but margin fell from 32% to 18%.
- Web projects are delivered 3 weeks late on average (previously, 1).
- Two large clients complained about "too many rounds of changes."
- Two junior people were hired, and a new project management tool was adopted.
- The director believes "the problem is that the juniors are slow" and proposes to stop hiring juniors.
- An AI assistant the director consulted claimed that "agencies lose an average of 30% of their margin to scope changes" (no source).

Additional data available (you may assume reasonable values and state them as assumptions): hours logged per project, number of change rounds per project, proposals signed with or without a detailed scope, hiring dates and the tool's adoption date.

## Deliverables

1. **Report (2,500–4,000 words)** with these sections:
   1. Executive summary (max. 250 words): problem, most likely cause, recommendation, level of confidence.
   2. Problem definition: symptoms vs. problem; complete statement; scope and constraints.
   3. Breakdown: issue tree (MECE).
   4. Diagnosis: at least 4 falsifiable hypotheses, prioritization, evidence for and against each one, root cause analysis (five whys or Ishikawa), correlation vs. causation.
   5. Evaluation of arguments and sources: analysis of at least one stakeholder argument (fallacies, assumptions), possible biases (including your own), verification of at least one source or AI output.
   6. Options: at least 3, including "do nothing."
   7. Decision: classification by reversibility and impact, weighted matrix or EV with explicit calculations and sensitivity analysis, pre-mortem of the chosen option.
   8. Action plan: steps, owners, metrics, success and kill criteria, review date.
   9. Assumptions and limitations.
2. **Decision log** for the recommended decision (format from Unit 5, section 6).
3. **Verification appendix:** table of factual claims used · source · status.

## Assessment

### Automatic part — checklist (20%)
Verifiable by script or structural review (yes/no; 2 points each):
1. Length between 2,500 and 4,000 words.
2. Executive summary of 250 words or fewer.
3. All nine sections present with their headings.
4. At least 4 numbered hypotheses.
5. At least 3 options, one of them "do nothing."
6. A matrix with weights adding up to 100%, or an EV calculation, present.
7. A pre-mortem with at least 5 causes.
8. A plan with a metric and a review date.
9. A decision log with every field.
10. A verification appendix with at least 3 rows.

### Human part — rubric (80%)

| Criterion (weight) | Excellent (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Insufficient (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Definition and breakdown (15%)** | Separates symptoms from problem precisely; complete statement; MECE tree | Correct statement; tree with minor overlaps | Partly confuses symptom and problem | Problem poorly defined |
| **Diagnosis and root cause (25%)** | Falsifiable, prioritized hypotheses; evidence both ways; systemic root cause; handles correlation/causation | Good hypotheses; evidence mostly supporting | Few or poorly framed hypotheses | Jumps to the solution |
| **Critical thinking (20%)** | Spots fallacies and assumptions; acknowledges own biases; verifies sources/AI rigorously | Spots fallacies and verifies one source | Superficial analysis | Missing |
| **Decision (20%)** | Correct calculations; sensitivity; reversibility considered; useful pre-mortem that changes the plan | Correct matrix/EV without sensitivity | Calculation errors or after-the-fact criteria | Decision with no method |
| **Plan and communication (20%)** | Actionable plan with metrics and kill criteria; clear writing for the decision-maker; explicit uncertainty | Clear plan, partial metrics | Vague plan | No plan |

**Project grade** = checklist (20%) + weighted rubric scaled to 80%. At least 60% is required to pass the course.

**Grader's guide (North Studio case):** a strong answer doesn't accept the director's hypothesis without evidence (ad hominem/generalization about the juniors), examines the timing overlap between the tool and the new hires (confounding), explores poorly defined scope in proposals and change rounds as a likely cause, and doesn't use the AI's 30% figure without a source. There's no single right answer: the process is what's assessed.

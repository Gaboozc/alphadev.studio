# Unit 4 — Quiz

Key in `../claves/u4-clave-cuestionario.md`. **Part A [AUTO]** · **Part B [HUMAN]**

1. System 1 is:
   a) Always wrong. b) Fast and intuitive. c) A type of software. d) Slow and deliberate.
2. Tversky and Kahneman's 1974 article described the heuristics of:
   a) Confirmation, survivorship and sunk cost. b) Attention, interest, desire and action (AIDA). c) Representativeness, availability and anchoring. d) Stop, investigate, find and trace.
3. The best countermeasure to confirmation bias is:
   a) Ignoring the data that doesn't fit the idea. b) Asking your boss's opinion before deciding. c) Defining in advance what data would change your mind. d) Reading faster to review more sources at once.
4. "Where are the ones who failed?" counters:
   a) Availability bias. b) Anchoring bias. c) The equivocation fallacy. d) Survivorship bias.
5. The question "If I were starting today, would I invest in this?" counters:
   a) The sunk cost fallacy. b) Representativeness bias. c) Anchoring bias. d) Overconfidence.
6. Lateral reading means:
   a) Leaving the page and seeing what others say about it. b) Skimming diagonally to get the gist quickly. c) Reading only the page's headings and subheadings. d) Reading the whole page, top to bottom, carefully.
7. In CRAAP, the "P" asks about:
   a) The source's popularity. b) The page where it's published. c) The price of access to the source. d) The source's purpose.
8. A link cited by an AI:
   a) Doesn't matter if the text is coherent. b) Must always be opened and checked. c) Is a primary, reliable source. d) Always exists, even if you don't open it.
9. Verification effort should match:
   a) Nothing: everything is verified in equal detail. b) The free time you have that day. c) The total length of the text being reviewed. d) The risk of the data being wrong.
10. Knowing about biases:
    a) Isn't enough; you need processes. b) Only helps you see them in others. c) Makes us immune to them. d) Is useless, because they're unconscious.

# 02 · Case: "Altiplano Café: Why Are Subscribers Leaving?"

**Id:** `s0-c2-u2-caso` · **Format:** decision-point case, in the style of the Harvard Business School case method: one protagonist, incomplete information and a decision to make and defend (https://www.hbs.edu/mba/academic-experience/the-case-method) · **Level:** intermediate · **Time:** approx. 90 min of individual prep, 20 min in a study group and 40 min of discussion  · **Assessment:** reviewed with a rubric, with the rubric in section 7.

**Objectives:**
- *Analyze* a problem with several possible causes and separate them with data.
- *Evaluate* the quality of each source of evidence.
- *Create* a recommendation with a test that decides between the hypotheses.

> **Fictional case.** Altiplano Café, its people, its suppliers and all its figures are invented for teaching. Any resemblance to real companies is coincidental. The case is set in Mexico, so amounts are in Mexican pesos (MXN).

---

## 1. Friday, 6:40 p.m.

Daniela Ríos closed the subscriptions dashboard and stared at the curve. In August, Altiplano Café was gaining about 25 net subscribers a week: from 2,010 on August 4 it reached 2,123 by mid-September. Since then it has lost about 20 a week, and on October 26 it closed at **2,003**. If nothing changes, the monthly subscription for specialty coffee roasted in Puebla will lose close to 90 customers a month.

The message from Andrés Pazos, the founder, had arrived an hour earlier:

> "Dani, on Monday at 9 I want an answer: what's going on and what do we do? I have MXN $60,000 this month for whatever you propose. I think it was the price increase, so let's roll it back and be done."

Daniela had been growth manager for eight months. She knew that in September **four things** had changed almost at once. And everyone on the team had their own explanation.

## 2. The company

- Altiplano sells whole-bean or ground coffee by monthly subscription: 2 bags of 250 g, shipped on the first Tuesday of each month. New subscribers also join every week, with their first delivery within 5 days.
- **Price:** MXN $289 a month through August 31 and **MXN $329** from September 1. The reason was the rising cost of green coffee. Existing subscribers were notified by email on August 25.
- **Customers:** about 30% live in Mexico City and 70% in the rest of the country.
- **Acquisition:** Meta ads, with a fixed budget of MXN $30,000 a month, plus organic traffic (Instagram, referrals and search).

## 3. What changed in September

| Date | Change |
|---|---|
| Sep 1 (week 5) | The price goes up from $289 to $329 for everyone |
| Sep 1 (week 5) | According to the ads dashboard, Meta's cost per thousand impressions (CPM) rises about 25% compared with August |
| Sep 8 (week 6) | **Change of shipping carrier outside Mexico City**: from *Ruta Exprés* to *EnvíaYa*, $18 cheaper per shipment. In Mexico City, the usual local courier stays |
| Sep 15 (week 7) | New packaging (compostable bag and smaller box) for everyone |
| Sep 22 (week 8) | The cancellation survey is redesigned: it used to be an open question and is now a list of options with "Price" first |

## 4. What the team says

- **Andrés (founder):** "It's the price. We raised it 40 pesos, and people feel it."
- **Mauricio (marketing):** "Meta got really expensive. With the same budget we bring in fewer people. If we double the spend, we'll recover."
- **Lucía (operations):** "EnvíaYa saves us about $25,000 a month. There are some complaints, but there are always complaints."
- **Rafa (customer service):** "A lot of people say the coffee arrives late. And several didn't like the new box: they say it arrives crushed."

## 6. Your task

### 6.1 Individual prep (approx. 90 min)
Answer in writing: 600–900 words in total; a table counts as text.

1. **The problem:** state it with the U1 template. Is there **one** problem or two (few sign-ups and many cancellations)?
2. **Hypotheses:** write at least 4, one for each September change, each with what would refute it.
3. **Separate the causes:** use exhibits 1 and 2 to decide which hypothesis explains the **cancellations** and which explains the **sign-ups**.
   - *Hint, no answer:* look in the data for a group that received some changes and not others.
4. **The survey:** what can you conclude from "price" going from 15% to 46%? Justify it with the evidence hierarchy in the guide (section 5).
5. **The comments:** what value do they have as evidence, and what are they good for?
6. **Recommendation to Andrés:** what you do with the $60,000 and why. Include a test that could **refute** your main hypothesis, with its metric, assignment, duration and what result would change your mind.

### 6.2 Study group (approx. 20 min, 3–4 people)
Before the discussion, compare your answers to question 3. **You don't need to agree**; write down where you disagree and why.

### 6.3 Discussion (approx. 40 min, with an instructor or mentor)
Discussion plan (for the facilitator):
1. **(5 min)** What would Andrés do on Monday if nobody told him anything? Quick vote among A, B, C and D.
2. **(10 min)** What's the problem? One or two?
3. **(15 min)** What explains the cancellations? What role does Mexico City play in the analysis?
4. **(5 min)** The survey: data or artifact?
5. **(5 min)** Final vote. Who changed their mind and why?

## 7. Rubric (4 criteria × 4 levels)

| Criterion | 4 · Outstanding | 3 · Proficient | 2 · Developing | 1 · Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Separating causes** | Distinguishes the two problems and uses a comparison group to attribute each one; quantifies | Distinguishes the two problems and attributes causes with data, without quantifying | Mentions several causes but doesn't separate them with data | Picks one cause without testing it |
| **Quality of evidence** | Identifies the survey artifact and the limited value of anecdotes, and justifies it with the hierarchy | Identifies one of the two evidence problems | Treats all evidence as equally valid | Relies on the survey or the anecdotes as proof |
| **Proposed test** | Feasible randomized or comparative test, with metric, duration and refutation criterion | Reasonable test missing one element | Vague test ("see what happens") | Doesn't propose a test |
| **Recommendation** | Allocates resources consistently with the analysis, acknowledges uncertainty and risk | Consistent with the analysis | Partly consistent | Doesn't follow from the analysis |

Passing is ≥ 10/16 (the same 60% criterion as the course project, rounded). The teaching note is in `claves/02-nota-docente-caso.md`.

# Teaching Note · Altiplano Café Case

**Use:** for instructors, mentors or reviewers only. The whole case is fictional.

## What the case is designed to teach
1. **Two problems, not one:** sign-ups go down and cancellations go up. They have different causes.
2. **A natural comparison group:** Mexico City got the price increase, the new packaging and the new survey, but **not** the carrier change.
3. **A change in measurement:** the survey went from an open question to a list with "Price" first.
4. **Anecdotes vs. data:** the comments are useful for generating hypotheses, not for confirming them.

## Reference analysis

**Cancellations**
- **Mexico City:** 14.25 per week in W1–W4 and 14.75 in W5–W12. Practically the same.
- **Rest of the country:** 21 per week in W1–W5. From W6 it rises to 34, 45, 52 and levels off around 55.
- **The price** went up in W5 for everyone, and cancellations didn't change in Mexico City or the rest of the country during W5 (23 vs. approx. 21). → Price **doesn't explain** the rise in cancellations.
- **The packaging** changed in W7 for everyone, and Mexico City didn't move. → Packaging **doesn't explain** the cancellations; at most, it caused annoyance in a few comments.
- **The carrier** changed in W6 only outside Mexico City and coincides with the jump. Delivery days went from 2.1 to 4.3, and "arrived late" tickets from 8 to 31 per week. There's a plausible mechanism, the right time order and a control group (Mexico City). → **It's the main hypothesis for the cancellations.**
- **The gradual rise** (34 → 45 → 52 → 55) is consistent with each customer experiencing their first delay on their next monthly shipment [INFERENCE from the case design; students are expected to notice it and raise it as a hypothesis].

**Sign-ups**
- **Ads:** 37.5 per week in W1–W4 and 29.5 in W5–W12, 21% fewer.
- **Organic:** 22.5 per week in W1–W4 and 18.75 in W5–W12, 17% fewer.
- **Organic sign-ups don't depend on CPM** and still fall from W5. → The price probably reduces sign-ups.
- **Ad sign-ups fall slightly more than organic ones.** → Consistent with price plus CPM. It can't be fully separated with this data.

**The survey**
- It isn't comparable between August and October: the instrument changed, and so did the order of the options.
- The 46% for "price" doesn't prove that price causes the cancellations. Besides, Mexico City cancellations, which didn't change, would also tick "price."
- The right move is to declare it **not comparable** and, if anything, go back to an open question or rotate the order of the options.

**Subscriber balance** (matches exhibit 1)
- 2,010 + 626 sign-ups − 633 cancellations = 2,003.
- Weekly net balance: +25 in W1–W4, +13 in W5, +1 in W6 and between −19 and −24 from W8.
- The trend shift is about 45 subscribers a week. Of these, about 30 come from cancellations outside Mexico City (approx. 21 → approx. 54) and about 12 from the drop in sign-ups (approx. 60 → approx. 48).
- **Conclusion for the discussion:** the leak from cancellations weighs more than twice as much as the drop in sign-ups.

## Reference recommendation (others are acceptable)
- **Don't roll back the price yet (option A).** It's the most expensive option (approx. $80,000 a month), and the evidence indicates it does **not** cause the cancellations. Its effect on sign-ups exists but is moderate.
- **Test the carrier (D applied to B).** For 4–6 weeks, randomly assign half the ZIP codes outside Mexico City to Ruta Exprés and the other half to EnvíaYa. See below for how it's measured and what it decides.
- **Don't double the ads while there's a leak (C).** Pouring customers into a leaky bucket doesn't fix the problem. Review the CPM in a separate analysis.
- **Fix the survey:** open question, or a list with options in random order.
- **Use of the $60,000:** cover the test's extra cost (approx. $12,600 a month on half the shipments) plus a retention incentive for customers who had delays. Hold the rest until there are results.

**The test's metric and decision criterion**
- **Primary metric:** 30-day cancellation rate by group.
- **Secondary metrics:** days to delivery and tickets.
- **Refutation criterion:** if cancellations in the Ruta Exprés group don't clearly drop compared with the EnvíaYa group, the carrier hypothesis loses strength. You'd then look at packaging or the interaction between price and delay.

## Common mistakes and how to redirect them in discussion
- **"It's the price, look at the survey."** → Ask: "What happened to Mexico City cancellations when the price went up?"
- **"It's everything at once."** → Ask: "Which week does each series start to jump?"
- **"Let's go back to Ruta Exprés and be done."** It's a reasonable decision, but without a test nothing is learned. → Ask: "What would you do if it doesn't go down?"

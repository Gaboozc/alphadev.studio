## Level 2: reference analysis
- **Iván:** **ruled out**. His shift's rate is the same as Rosa's, and on WhatsApp, which he also packs, it didn't go up at all.
- **New menu:** **ruled out as the main cause**. It affects both channels, but WhatsApp didn't change. It might add some complexity, though there's no sign of it.
- **One-tap button:** **likely as the explanation for the rise in complaints**. It's the only change that affected just one channel, and that channel is the only one that went up.
- **Creates or reveals?** The audit finds approx. 4% incomplete orders in **both** channels, above WhatsApp complaints (1.5%) and close to app complaints after the button (5.0%). → The button **reveals** a problem that already existed; it doesn't create it. The real problem has been there a while ("it's always happened," according to Rosa).
- **Acceptable additional hypothesis:** the driver doesn't check the bag. Still alive as a contributing factor.
- **Five whys** (reference):
  1. Why are drinks missing? Because the order goes out before the drink arrives (Rosa).
  2. Why does it go out first? Because the drink is added last, at another station, and there's no checkpoint before the bag is sealed (Rosa, Toño).
  3. Why is there no checkpoint? Because the counter's ticket is separate from the packing ticket, and nobody sees the complete order (Toño, Iván).
  4. Why does nobody see the complete order? Because there's no packing procedure or closing checklist (Iván: "nobody explained an order to me").
  5. **Systemic cause:** there's no order-closing procedure that brings the two stations together.
- **Answer to Sofía:** don't move Iván. His shift has the same rate as Rosa's. The problem is the process, and it existed before he arrived; now we simply see it.

## Level 3: reference design
- **Solution:** a bag-closing checklist plus a "no sealing without the drink" rule: the counter hands over drinks labeled with the order number.
- **Assignment:** by **day**, at random. For example, over 3 weeks, 10 days with the checklist and 11 without. Not by order, because the same staff can't follow two procedures at once without contaminating them. Not by shift either, because it would be confounded with the person.
- **Primary metric:** the percentage of incomplete orders in a **random audit**, for example 15 orders a day per channel. **Not** complaints, because the channels report differently.
- **Drop criterion:** if the audit rate on checklist days doesn't clearly fall compared with non-checklist days; for example, if the difference is smaller than the day-to-day variation.
- **Measurement:** add an automatic WhatsApp message after delivery asking "Did everything arrive? Yes / No," to match the app's button.
- **Memo criteria:** conclusion first ("the rise in complaints is because we now measure better; the real problem already existed, and it isn't Iván"), evidence in 3 points, recommendation and a review date.

**Typical mistakes:**
- Blaming Iván because "the dates line up."
- Concluding that "there's no problem, it's just the button." The problem exists: approx. 4% of orders are incomplete.
- Using complaints as the test metric.

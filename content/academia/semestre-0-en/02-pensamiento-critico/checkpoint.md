## Part B — Applied case: the coffee shop [fictional case] (reviewed with a rubric)

An online specialty coffee shop sold 18% less over the last 6 weeks. The owner says: "It's obvious the competition is stealing our customers; everyone in the industry is investing in ads, so we have to double the advertising budget right now." Data: traffic fell 3%; 7 weeks ago the shipping provider was changed; complaints about delays went from 4 to 19 a month.

**What you need to turn in** (350–500 words, 35–45 min):

1. Write the problem statement with all six elements; if a data point is missing, mark it as an assumption.
2. Write three falsifiable hypotheses and, for each one, the observation that would disprove it.
3. Identify a fallacy or bias in the owner's reasoning and respond without dismissing him.
4. Recommend what to do first, using reversibility and a brief pre-mortem (two causes of failure).

**AI policy:** you may use AI to generate ideas, not to write the analysis. If you used it, say so in one line at the end.

### Rubric (12 points; visible from the start)

| Criterion | Excellent (3) | Adequate (2) | Developing (1) | Insufficient (0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Statement | All six elements with case data and assumptions marked | Four or five elements | Two or three, or mixes in the solution | Missing |
| Hypotheses and evidence | Three falsifiable hypotheses, each with its test, using the data (traffic, shipping, complaints) | Two good hypotheses, or vague tests | Unfalsifiable hypotheses | Missing |
| Reasoning | Correctly names the fallacy or bias (e.g., appeal to popularity or confirmation) and responds with questions | Names it but responds with a label | Confuses it | Missing |
| Decision | Recommendation consistent with reversibility, a pilot or test, and a pre-mortem with two concrete causes | Reasonable recommendation, partially justified | Recommendation with no method | Missing |

Part B is passed with at least 7/12 .
